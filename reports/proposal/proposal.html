Forecasting Top-Performing Israeli Provident Funds and Discovering Their Risk–Return Profiles

Roy Ashkenazi  ·  Data Mining Course Project 2026

GitHub: https://github.com/RoidoAsh/gemelnet-ml-project

1. Dataset Selection

Gemel-Net, published by the Capital Market, Insurance and Savings Authority on data.gov.il: https://data.gov.il/he/datasets/cma/gemelnet. It contains the monthly reports of every Israeli provident fund, study fund and severance fund track. After merging its three files, the data has 187,665 monthly records of 1,938 funds, from January 1999 to October 2026. Each record has 31 columns: cash flows, total assets, management fees, returns over several horizons, risk measures (standard deviation, Sharpe ratio, Alpha), liquidity, market exposures, and the fund's type, specialization, managing company and target population.

2. Motivation

Israeli savers choose among hundreds of fund tracks, usually by past returns, which are a weak guide to the future. We ask two questions. (1) Prediction: can a fund's reported profile at the end of a year (fees, risk, cash flows, size, exposures and recent returns) predict whether it will be in the top quartile of its fund type next year, better than simply picking last year's winners? (2) Structure: do funds actually behave according to their declared specialization, or do data-driven risk–return profiles reveal different groups? The answers are useful to savers, advisors and the regulator.

3. Method

  • Data preparation. Merge the three files and fix the fund inception date, which uses a different format in each file. Use data from 2010 onward, since most measures are missing before then, and drop the deposit fee (80% missing). Risk measures are missing mainly for funds younger than 5 years, so we will add fund age and a missing-value indicator, and compare several imputation methods. We will also apply Z-score normalization, One-Hot encoding of categorical variables, and IQR-based outlier detection.
  • Feature engineering. Fund age, net cash flow relative to fund size, return relative to funds of the same type, returns of previous years, and stock and foreign exposure as a percentage of assets (the raw data reports them in ₪ millions). In total, more than 20 features.
  • Target variable. For each fund we take its December report of each year (11,855 December snapshots in total, of which 8,367 are from 2010–2024). The label is 1 if, in the following year, the fund's return was in the top 25% of funds of the same type, and 0 otherwise.
  • Classification (7 models). Logistic Regression, KNN, Decision Tree, SVM, Random Forest, XGBoost and a Neural Network. The hyperparameters of each model will be tuned with Grid Search or Randomized Search.
  • Clustering (4 models). K-Means, Hierarchical clustering (Ward), DBSCAN and GMM, applied to each fund's risk–return profile (Sharpe ratio, standard deviation, returns, % stocks, % foreign and fees). Results will be visualized in two dimensions with PCA and t-SNE. As an addition, we will run anomaly detection with LOF and Isolation Forest, to find funds that behave differently from similar funds.

4. Intended Experiments and Evaluation

  • Time-based split. Train on 2010–2020 (5,951 snapshots), validate on 2021–2022 (1,164) and test on 2023–2024 (1,252). A random split would cause data leakage, because the same funds and years would appear in both training and testing. Funds that closed have no next-year label (survivorship bias); we will measure this effect.
  • Classification metrics. Confusion matrix, Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1, Balanced Accuracy, the ROC curve and AUC, and the Precision–Recall curve. The key metric is Precision: the share of funds recommended by the model that actually reach the top quartile. Models will be compared with two baselines: a random pick (25% success) and picking last year's winners.
  • Experiments. (1) Compare all classification models. (2) Handle class imbalance: class weights vs. SMOTE vs. Tomek Links. (3) Compare imputation methods. (4) Feature importance: which characteristics drive the prediction most. (5) Error analysis by fund type and by year. (6) Test whether adding the fund's cluster as a feature improves classification.
  • Clustering evaluation. Choose the number of clusters with the elbow method (SSE), the Silhouette score and a dendrogram, and compare the models by Silhouette score. We will also use the Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) to measure how well the clusters match the funds' declared specialization.
