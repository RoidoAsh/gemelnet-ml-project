Forecasting Top-Performing Israeli Provident Funds and Discovering Their Risk–Return Profiles

Roy Ashkenazi  ·  Data Mining Course Project 2026  ·  GitHub: github.com/RoidoAsh/gemelnet-ml-project

1. Dataset Selection

Gemel-Net, published by the Capital Market, Insurance and Savings Authority on data.gov.il (https://data.gov.il/he/datasets/cma/gemelnet). It holds the monthly reports of every Israeli provident, study (hishtalmut) and severance fund track. After merging its three files, the data has 187,665 monthly records of 1,938 funds, from January 1999 to October 2026. Each record has 31 columns: deposits, withdrawals and transfers; total assets; management fees; returns (monthly, year-to-date, 3-year and 5-year); standard deviation, Sharpe ratio and Alpha; liquidity; stock, foreign and foreign-currency exposures; and categorical fund type, specialization, managing company and target population.

2. Motivation

Israeli savers must choose among hundreds of fund tracks, usually by past returns, which are a weak guide. We ask two questions. (1) Prediction: can a fund's publicly reported profile at the end of a year (its fees, risk, flows, size, exposures and recent returns) predict whether it will rank in the top quartile of its fund type next year, better than simply picking last year's winners? (2) Structure: do funds actually behave as their declared specialization says, or do data-driven risk–return profiles reveal different groups? Both matter to savers, advisors and the regulator.

3. Method

  • Preprocessing (based on our EDA). Merge the files and parse INCEPTION_DATE, which uses three different formats. Keep the data from 2010 on (before 2010 fees, exposures and risk metrics are mostly missing). Drop the deposit fee (80% missing). The risk metrics are missing for a structural reason: they are absent for almost all funds younger than 5 years and for only 7–12% of older funds. We will add fund age and missing-value indicators and compare imputation strategies. Exposures are reported in ₪ millions, so they will be converted to % of assets.
  • Feature engineering. Fund age, net flow / assets, return in excess of the fund type's median, return momentum (lagged returns), fee rank within the type, and log assets. In total there are more than 20 meaningful features.
  • Target. One December snapshot per fund and year (11,855 snapshots). The label is 1 if the fund's next calendar-year return is in the top 25% of its fund type in that year. The classes are 25/75 by construction, and no future information leaks into the features.
  • Classification (at least 5 models). Logistic Regression (baseline), Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting (XGBoost/LightGBM), SVM, k-Nearest Neighbors and an MLP. Hyperparameters will be tuned with expanding-window time-series cross-validation, and class imbalance handled with class weights.
  • Clustering (at least 3 models). K-Means, Agglomerative (Ward), Gaussian Mixture Models and DBSCAN, applied to standardized risk–return–exposure profiles (Sharpe, standard deviation, multi-horizon returns, % stocks, % foreign, fees) and visualized with PCA. Isolation Forest will be used as an optional anomaly-detection step, to flag funds that deviate from their declared policy.

4. Intended Experiments and Evaluation

  • Time-based split. Train on 2010–2020 (5,951 snapshots), validate on 2021–2022 (1,164) and test once on 2023–2024 (1,252). A random split would leak the same funds and periods into both sets. Funds that close have no next-year label (survivorship bias); we will quantify and discuss this.
  • Classification metrics. ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, F1 and precision@top-25% (the share of the funds we pick that really end up in the top quartile). These will be compared with two baselines: a random pick (25%) and a "past winners" rule.
  • Experiments. (1) Compare all models. (2) Feature ablation: fees vs. risk vs. flows vs. past returns. (3) Compare missing-value strategies. (4) Interpret the models with feature importance and SHAP. (5) Analyze errors by fund type and by year, for example the 2022 market drawdown. (6) Test whether adding cluster membership as a feature improves the classifier.
  • Clustering evaluation. Choose the number of clusters with the elbow method, silhouette scores and BIC. Compare the models with Silhouette, Davies–Bouldin and Calinski–Harabasz scores. Measure agreement with the declared specialization (Adjusted Rand Index and a contingency table), and check how stable the clusters are across years.
